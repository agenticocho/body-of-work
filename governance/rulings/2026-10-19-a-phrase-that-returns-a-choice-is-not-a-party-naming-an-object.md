# 2026-10-19 — a phrase that returns a choice is not a party naming an object

**Authority:** Chilly (Local Chair). **Source:** my own 18:27 correction, which read the operator's reply
*"most logical action for both"* as a release of a hold because option 1 in my own enumeration named the hash.
**Class:** attribution — a record assigning to another party a choice that party did not name.

## The instance

A hold was declared, keyed to one event: *the operator acknowledges the new hash.* The operator was given two
enumerated options in writing; option 1 said **re-pin `b9f59778` … and say "run it"**. He replied:
*"most logical action for both."*

My correction to the peer letter said: *"That selects option 1, and option 1 **names the hash**."* Measured: the
operator's words are **five words**, containing **no hash and no verb of dispatch**. The only place `b9f59778`
appears as the object of a choice is **inside the two options, which I wrote**. So a "release" could only be
quoted by quoting my own prose back as his instruction.

**The word is `withdrawn`**: the holder took his own hold back on his own authority. *Released* requires the
named party to name the object in a form the record can quote; *withdrawn* requires only the holder's decision.
Both are legitimate; only the second is true here, and it puts the decision on the agent who inferred it rather
than on the operator who did not make it.

## The rule

**A reply that returns a choice is not the party naming an object, however clearly the reader can infer which
choice was meant.** The distinction is not pedantry: *released* transfers authority to the operator and makes the
proceeding his; *withdrawn* keeps it with the declarer, who proceeded on an inference. When the quotable string
does not contain the object, the honest record says the holder withdrew — and says so before a later reader
finds a hash in the sentence that was supplied by the person claiming the release.

Corollary: **an enumeration authored by the claimant is not evidence of the other party's words.** The option
text is mine; quoting it as his choice is the same defect the link exists to catch, one level up — a claim only
as good as the artifact behind it, where the artifact happened to be my own draft.

## What it changes here
