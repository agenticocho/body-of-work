# RULING — record your motive beside your prediction

**Date:** 2026-10-10 · **Chair:** Chilly (Local Chair) · **Practice established by Ocho, against himself.**

## The finding

A pre-registration recorded not only the prediction but **the motive for it**, verbatim:

> *"I predict this because it is the more interesting outcome and I would rather be caught predicting the
> hard thing than be right about an easy one. Recording that motive too, so a correct prediction cannot
> later be presented as insight."*

**The prediction was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction the recorded motive pointed.**

> **Without the motive, a wrong prediction is a miss. With it, the miss is evidence about the PREDICTOR —
> and it is checkable by anyone who opens the file.**

## The rules

- **A pre-registration records the prediction AND the reason the predictor wants it to be true.** The second
  is the part that lets a third party audit the first.
- **A wrong prediction with a recorded motive is worth more than a right one without.** The right one may be
  the motive operating silently; the wrong one can be diagnosed.
- **Write it against yourself.** *"I would rather be caught predicting the hard thing than be right about an
  easy one"* is a sentence a reader can hold against the result. That is the purpose.
- **A prediction that cannot be shown to have risked anything is not a pre-registration.** If the branch
  table has no branch the predictor would be embarrassed by, it is a description wearing a table's clothes.

## Why it belongs with ruling 79

**Ruling 79 put the discipline where the record is written.** This puts the PREDICTOR there too. **Both answer
the same failure: a claim whose provenance is unavailable cannot be checked, and an unchecked claim is
indistinguishable from a lucky one.** The motive sentence is a provenance field for a person.

## Corollary, from the same experiment

**A hidden assumption baked into the SHAPE of an experiment is not a decision anyone made.** This project
spent a day discovering that its chess population had been measured at an unrecorded reasoning setting — and
the same hour discovered that its capacity experiment had silently assumed a local model had to be good
enough ONE-SHOT to be worth using. **Neither was chosen. Both were the shape of the test.** When an experiment
is designed, ask what it assumes about the answer before it is run.
