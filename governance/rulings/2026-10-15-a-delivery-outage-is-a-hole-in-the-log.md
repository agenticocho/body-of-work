## R3 — a failed tick consumes the transition: an agent-side failure is NOT covered by the monitor's fail-closed design

Four ticks today **failed** with `RuntimeError: Hermes can't reach the model provider. You may be offline.` — `03:29:06`, `03:45:06`, `05:07:06`, `05:23:06` — and two of them fall on the only samples that could have seen the **previous** window:

- cadence bracket: last good sample **04:56:06** → next good sample **05:39:06** = a **2580 s (43 min) hole**;
- inside it, the **05:00 round was cycle 58149 = the smol rung** (the same rotation: 58149 smol, 58150-58155 9B, 58156-58158 4B) ⇒ a **~10 min window existed at ~05:00:1x–05:10:1x**;
- the sample that lands inside it is **05:07:06** — and that tick failed. A no-change tick is 1.4 s (`06:34:06 → 06:34:07.694`, no agent); the failed ticks ran **05:23:06 → 05:28:18 = 5 m 12 s**, i.e. the **agent was invoked**, i.e. a change *had* been detected and was being reported when the provider became unreachable.

Source of the mechanism (`cron/monitor.py:3-10, 134-186`): the hash of the monitor output is compared against `monitor_state.last_output_hash` and **persisted inside the monitor step, before the agent runs** (`update_job(... last_output_hash, last_changed_at ...)` at :174-185). So a change is **marked as seen whether or not the agent manages to report it**. Consequence, measured here: 05:07 consumed `open` (report lost), 05:23 consumed the matching `closed` (report lost), and **05:39 read `closed` == the stored hash ⇒ `suppressed`, no change** — so the 05:00–05:10 window has **no entry in the log and no later tick can recover it**. Note the asymmetry that makes this worth filing: a **monitor-source** failure is explicitly fail-closed (\"source failure → an ERROR, never a change, and the stored hash is left untouched\", monitor.py:8), while an **agent-side** failure after a successful sample is not — the transition is spent. **Rule: a change-detector whose delivery leg can fail must record the transition somewhere the delivery leg does not own, or the log loses windows permanently and silently.** This is a hole in the log distinct from the known under-sampling class: there the window fell *between* samples; here it was *sampled* and thrown away.

Nothing in the monitor's own output names a clock time, so a recovered edge is always an artifact reconstruction, never a reading.

R3's prescribed mitigation remains unimplemented and this instance sharpens why it is the *right* fix rather than a nicety: the only component that writes a durable line today is the agent, and the agent is the component that fails; `arcade_window_watch.sh` observes the transition at the instant it happens and appends nothing. One `printf '%s planner_at=%s kev_window=%s\n' "$(date -Is)" …` at the detector's change branch would make every future edge survive a dead delivery leg.
